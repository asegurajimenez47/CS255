# CS255 SNHU CS 255 System and Analysis and Design Portfolio

## DriverPass Project Summary
The DriverPass project was about designing a system for a driving school that wanted to help students prepare for their DMV driving tests. The client wanted a system where customers could create accounts, schedule driving lessons, take online practice tests, and access training materials. The system also needed to let employees manage customer information and appointments while giving the owner and IT staff access to the features they needed.

## What I Did Well
I think I did a good job of breaking down the client's needs and turning them into requirements and system designs. I identified the different users and what each person needed to do in the system. I also worked on diagrams to show how users would interact with the system and how the different parts would work together.

## What I Would Revise
If I could revise one part of my work, I would spend more time reviewing my diagrams and making sure they clearly match the requirements in my documents. I would also look for ways to make them easier for someone who is not technical to understand. This would help avoid confusion and make the design easier to follow during development.

## Interpreting the User's Needs
I used the information provided by the DriverPass client to figure out what the system needed to do. For example, customers needed to schedule lessons and take practice tests, while employees needed to manage appointments and customer records. I used those needs to plan the system's features and create diagrams showing how the users would interact with it. Considering the user's needs is important because the system is supposed to solve the client's actual problems. If those needs are misunderstood, the finished system might not work the way the client expects.

## My Approach to Software Design
When designing software, I would start by understanding the problem and gathering the requirements before deciding how the system should work. I would identify the users, their responsibilities, and the features they need. Then I would use diagrams, such as use case and activity diagrams, to organize the design and show how the system fits together. In the future, I would compare the design against the original requirements and get feedback before moving forward. This would help me catch problems early and make sure the final system meets the user's needs.
